Bernoulli’s equation for ideal flow (steady, no friction, constant density):

Ps + Pdyn = Pt   with Pdyn = ½ρv²
Ps = static, Pdyn = dynamic, Pt = total pressure. Pt stays constant along a streamline. The venturi is horizontal, so the height term ρgz does not change and is left out.

Bernoulli between points 1 and 2, plus continuity (Q = A₁v₁ = A₂v₂), gives the ideal flow rate:

Qideal = A₂ × √[ 2ΔP / ρ(1 − (A₂/A₁)²) ]
ΔP = P₁ − P₂ = ρg(h₁ − h₂) is the pressure drop from inlet to throat. Throat area A₂ = 78.5 mm², and A₁/A₂ = 4.
The coefficient of discharge is Cd = Qactual / Qideal. It shows how much real friction reduces the flow.

Static pressure field (Ps)

Blue = lowest Ps. Red = highest Ps.

Observe:
  • Inlet: red. Ps is highest and the water moves slowly.
  • Throat: blue. Ps is lowest and the water moves fastest.
  • Outlet: Ps rises again, but not back to the inlet value. Friction takes some of it.

Velocity field

Blue = slowest. Red = fastest.

Observe:
  • The fastest water (red) is in the middle of the throat.
  • The water next to the wall is much slower (blue). Friction at the wall holds it back. Engineers call this region the boundary layer.
  • This slower region is thicker in the diverging section.
  • The equation Q = A × v assumes all the water at a cross-section moves at the same speed. Here it does not.
Observe
Q1 — Look at the velocity field in the throat. How does the speed of the water change from the middle to the wall?

Total pressure field (Pt)

Blue = lowest Pt. Red = highest Pt. If Bernoulli held everywhere, this map would be one colour.

Observe:
  • Inlet and converging section: one uniform red. Pt is constant, so Bernoulli works.
  • Throat and diverging section: red in the middle, but blue and green near the walls. Friction next to the wall removes energy, so Pt is lower there. Bernoulli does not work in this region.
  • Overall: Pt at the outlet is lower than at the inlet. This is the real friction loss.

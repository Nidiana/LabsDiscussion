Ohm's Law

Three problems. Every wrong option maps to a specific mistake, and the feedback names it.

Cover the quantity you want in the triangle above — tap V, I or R.

1A circuit is connected to a 12 V supply through a 4 Ω resistor. What current flows?

V = 12 V   R = 4 Ω   I = ?

Current is the unknown, so cover I in the triangle. What is left is V sitting above R, which means I = V / R. Check the units before you divide: volts per ohm must come out as amperes.
Substitute, but don't evaluate yet: I = 12 V ÷ 4 Ω. Both quantities are already in base SI units, so no conversion is needed here.
2A resistor draws 2 A when 10 V is placed across it. What is its resistance?

V = 10 V   I = 2 A   R = ?

Resistance is the unknown, so cover R. V remains above I, giving R = V / I. Watch the order: dividing current by voltage would give you conductance in siemens, not resistance.
Substitute without evaluating: R = 10 V ÷ 2 A. A useful sanity check — a bigger current for the same voltage must mean a smaller resistance.
3A device of resistance 6 Ω draws 500 mA. What voltage is across it?

R = 6 Ω   I = 500 mA   V = ?

Voltage is the unknown, so cover V. I and R sit side by side, which means they multiply: V = I × R. This one also carries a prefix — convert the current to amperes before anything else.
500 mA = 500 × 10−3 A = 0.5 A. Now substitute: V = 0.5 A × 6 Ω. Half an amp through a small resistance should give you a low voltage, not a large one.
